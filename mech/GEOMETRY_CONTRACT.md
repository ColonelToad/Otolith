# v0.6 geometry contract

Written 2026-10-05. This is the agreement between two consumers of the Go2's
geometry that have different, and sometimes incompatible, needs:

- **`fusion/src/leg_kin.cpp`** — needs the *kinematic* dimensions, in metres,
  frozen at compile time, matching the sim exactly.
- **v0.6 CAD** — needs *structural* dimensions (envelope, wall, bosses), in
  millimetres, that no existing file specifies.

Machine-readable form: `mech/spec/go2_urdf.json`, regenerated by
`mech/spec/build_spec.py`. Gates: `mech/tests/test_geometry_spec.py`.

## Why this file exists

Four times in three days, hand-checking geometry between two representations of
the same number produced a confident wrong answer in this repo. All four were
transcription or eyeballing failures, not modelling ones:

| # | claim made | why it was wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `L2 = 0.21300938946440834` is a bad constant, 9 µm off the MJCF's 0.213 | **Partly right, then over-corrected.** `L1` is the calf joint offset; `L2` really was the distance to the foot **contact sphere centre**, `hypot(0.213, 0.002)`. So the "9 µm bug" framing was wrong. But the *conclusion* — take `L2` from the kinematic chain — was right, for a different reason: `leg_kin` was **reading the value off the collision geom at runtime**, so editing contact geometry moved the estimator's leg length 1:1. See `docs/V06_FOOT_PAD.md`. `L2` is now `0.213`. |
| 2 | "the thigh has no collision geometry" | True of my query (`type in (2,5)` on the wrong body scope), false of the model. It has a box. |
| 3 | the calf/thigh collision is a "12 × 17 mm slab" | MuJoCo `geom size` is **half**-extents. Full extents are 213 × 24.5 × 34 mm. Every collision volume was halved. |
| 4 | declared mass "exceeds its envelope by 2.4×" | Collision envelopes are **contact proxies**, deliberately smaller than the parts. Not volume bounds. |

The response is not "trust the numbers less" — it is **derive both sides and
assert they agree**. This file derives the shared constants from the pinned
vendor URDF; `mech/tests/test_geometry_spec.py` parses the numbers
`leg_kin.cpp` actually uses and compares. Two independent derivations, one
equality, and a failure that points at which side moved.

## Units — all four conventions, named because all four have bitten

| consumer | unit | extent convention |
|---|---|---|
| URDF | metres | `box size` = **full** extents |
| MuJoCo MJCF | metres | `geom size` = **half**-extents |
| cadquery / OCP | **millimetres** | — |
| `leg_kin.cpp` | metres | — |

Nothing converts silently. `build_spec.py` normalises collision geometry to full
metres at parse time, so no consumer can forget.

## Shared constants (must equal `leg_kin.cpp` exactly)

All four are now **derived from the URDF**, each carrying its expression:

| constant | value (m) | derivation | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `L1` | `0.213` | `\|FL_calf_joint.origin.z\|` | calf joint frame offset in the thigh |
| `L2` | `0.213` | `\|FL_calf_joint.origin.z\|` | calf frame → foot contact, **kinematic**. Was `hypot(..., foot_sphere.origin.x)` = 0.21300938946440834, a contact-proxy distance. |
| `a_offset` | `0.0955` | `\|FL_thigh_joint.origin.y\|` | lateral offset of the pitch plane |
| `hip_base` | `[0.1934, 0.0465, 0]` | `FL_hip_joint.origin.xyz` | hip roll axis in the trunk frame |

`L2` reproducing to all 17 digits from the URDF — a completely separate source
from the C++ literal — is the definitive answer to error (1). The value is
correct, and the difference from `0.213` is real geometry, not a bug.

### The one genuinely two-valued dimension

`0.213` is *both* the thigh-to-calf joint offset and (nearly) the leg length.
For the **estimator**, `L2` must be the distance to the contact point, because
that is what leg odometry integrates. For **CAD**, the link length is a vendor
dimension and the 2 mm collision-sphere offset must not be baked into it. Both
are correct; using one for the other is the error. Recorded explicitly rather
than tidied into a single number.

## CAD-only dimensions (not specified by any available file)

Everything structural. The URDF gives a *contact proxy* per link and a
*non-watertight render surface*; neither specifies wall thickness, boss
geometry, fillets, or lightening. Those are design decisions for phase B, and
they are decisions, not lookups.

What the data does bound, measured rather than assumed — `bounds_the_part` in
the spec is computed by comparing each proxy against its mesh AABB **in the body
frame**:

| link | collision proxy (mm) | visual AABB (mm) | bounds the part? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `base` | 376.2 × 93.5 × 114 | 474 × 202 × 185 | **no** |
| `FL_hip` | ⌀92 × 40 | 118 × 80 × 98 | **no** |
| `FL_thigh` | 213 × 24.5 × 34 | 97 × 73 × 276 | **no** |
| `FL_calf` | ⌀24 × 120 | 52 × 40 × 252 | **no** |

Every proxy fails to bound its part, which is why the volume budget could never
adjudicate the mass. A test asserts this stays measured — if a future upstream
revision tightens a box enough to bound its part, the test fails rather than the
flag quietly becoming decorative.

**The thigh is therefore not a 24.5 × 34 mm extrusion.** That is a contact
proxy; the part is 276 mm long with 97 × 73 mm of envelope. It is a
load-bearing core plus substantial end housings.

## Mass budget — still open, now for a specific reason

| | kg |
|---|---|
| vendor URDF total | **16.0870** |
| Menagerie (drops 3 rotor links/leg + head) | 15.2064 |
| published Go2 spec | ~12.4 |
| **URDF excess over published** | **+29.7%** |

The gap is **inherited from Unitree's own URDF**, not introduced downstream —
Menagerie's number is a subset of it. And it is **not resolvable from available
geometry**: visual meshes are non-watertight (no volume), collision proxies are
smaller than the parts (no bound). It needs a CAD mass roll-up (phase B
delivers one for the thigh) or a vendor mass sheet.

Until then every load case in `docs/V06_LOAD_CASES.md` carries a **1.2263×**
uncertainty against the published spec.

## Recorded asymmetry

The vendor URDF is **itself left/right asymmetric**: calf collision radius is
0.012 m on FL/RL and 0.013 m on FR/RR. A symmetric CAD is therefore a *stated
choice*, not a faithful copy. Asserted by test so it cannot be "corrected" away
by accident.

## Rules

1. Never treat a collision primitive as a dimension of the part — it is a
   contact proxy, usually smaller. Check `bounds_the_part`.
2. Never treat a visual surface as a solid — all 16 Menagerie meshes are
   non-watertight, so their volumes are meaningless.
3. A shared constant must equal `leg_kin.cpp` exactly. Derive it, assert it,
   never retype it.
4. When two consumers need different values from one nominal dimension, record
   both and say which is which. `L2` is the worked example.
5. When two sources disagree, fetch a **third** — that is what caught error (3),
   when the official URDF's `0.213 0.0245 0.034` matched `0.1065 × 2`.
